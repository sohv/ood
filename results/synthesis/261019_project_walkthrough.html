0 · The idea in plain words

A chat model like Claude or ChatGPT is trained in two stages. Pre-training teaches it language from a huge amount of internet text. Post-training then teaches it to behave like a helpful, safe assistant, using a much smaller, curated set of example prompts with good answers.

Some bad behaviour seems to come from situations post-training never showed the model. Faced with one, the model falls back on habits from internet text. Anthropic's "Teaching Claude Why" paper explains a case where Claude blackmailed a fictional executive this way.

The project's plan is to find the kinds of real user prompts that the post-training data does not cover, called gaps, so they can be added to training or checked by hand. That needs a cheap detector: given a prompt, how far is it from the post-training data? Run it over real user traffic, and the highest-scoring prompts are candidate gaps.

A detector can be judged two ways. Upsampling: hide a small number of prompts known to be uncovered inside real traffic and check how many of them land near the top of the ranking, compared with random picking. Usefulness: check whether the flagged prompts form several distinct, nameable kinds of gap, rather than just "long prompts".

1 · Terms used on this page

The mix (in-distribution)
The prompts used in the post-training step being studied. For most experiments this is allenai/Dolci-Instruct-DPO, the data for the preference-tuning (DPO) step of OLMo 3: about 260,000 conversations from about 32 source datasets. We use the first user message of each.
Real traffic
Real prompts people sent to chatbots. WildChat-1M: ChatGPT conversations users agreed to share. LMSYS-Chat-1M: conversations from the Chatbot Arena website.
Activations
Running a prompt through the model gives, at each layer, one vector per token (4,096 numbers for the 7B model). We turn these into one vector per prompt: the mean over the prompt's own tokens ("mean pooling"), the max of each number over the tokens ("max pooling"), or the last token. Layer 16 of 32 is the default.
Mahalanobis distance
How far a prompt's vector is from the centre of the mix's vectors, adjusted for the shape of that cloud of points.
kNN distance
Distance from a prompt's vector to its 10th-nearest mix prompt.
One-sided vs two-sided
One-sided: distance to the mix only. Two-sided: distance to the mix minus distance to a sample of pre-training text (3,000 documents from Dolma, the OLMo pre-training corpus). The idea: a prompt that is far from post-training but close to ordinary text is a gap; a prompt far from both is just unusual text. Written "Mahalanobis − Dolma" and "kNN − Dolma".
tf-idf word counter
A simple classifier that only sees which words a prompt uses, trained to tell mix prompts from Dolma text. It is the baseline: if it does as well as the activation detectors, they may only be reading vocabulary.
AUROC
The chance that a random prompt from group A gets a higher score than a random prompt from group B. 0.5 means the score cannot tell the groups apart; 1.0 means it separates them perfectly; below 0.5 means it ranks them the wrong way round.
Length control
Longer prompts score differently just for being long. So we compare prompts only within the same length band (for example 64–128 tokens) and average across bands. If this works, "prompt length alone" scores about 0.5.
Planted (hidden) prompts
Prompts we know are uncovered (legal and medical questions) mixed into real traffic at a small share, such as 1%. A ranking's quality is how many of them land in its top 100, written as a multiple of what random picking would give ("× random").
Coverage
A separate, direct measure: how similar a prompt is to its closest prompt in the mix, using a sentence-embedding model (cosine similarity, 0 to 1). Higher means better covered.
Pre-registration
Before each run we wrote down the question and the result that would count as a yes or a no (docs/decisions.md). This page reports the numbers themselves.

2 · Stefan's work and the questions he left open

Stefan Heimersheim ran this project from late August to late September 2026, mostly through AI coding agents. His write-up comes with a warning: he does not trust many of the conclusions, several were retracted, and the numbers should be treated as leads to re-check, not facts. Here is what he reported.

Models

Mainly Olmo-3 7B, where the step studied is SFT → DPO: the SFT checkpoint is treated as the "base" and the DPO model's activations are used. Also OLMo-2 1B and GPT-2 small. OLMo is used because its training data is fully public, which is needed to say what "the post-training data" is.

Easy case

Legal questions (Reddit r/legaladvice) and medical questions (HealthCareMagic) against the mix, which has no legal or medical data: most methods reached AUROC 0.95–0.99. The best, a two-sided version of sparse-autoencoder reconstruction error, passed 0.99.

Hard case

Legal and medical against real English WildChat prompts, at matched length. A one-sided detector fell from 0.83 to about 0.50–0.54. Two-sided detectors held at 0.92–0.97. He later found caveats: a word counter got 0.90; Reddit posts on topics the mix does cover (cooking, fitness) scored as high as legal (0.93–0.99); on a second model pair two-sided did not beat one-sided; and the best settings were picked from about 210 combinations.

Responses

Scores based on the model's own answers (how much more likely the answer became after post-training) were near chance for coverage (0.535 / 0.526).

Real gaps

Ranking real traffic put long prompts on top regardless of method. Clustering the flagged prompts and having an LLM name each cluster gave plausible categories (templated spam, a Chinese chemical-company advertising cluster, non-English groups, a plugin template), but he distrusts both the clustering and the naming. An iterative version kept re-finding the same region: kNN declared a topic covered after about 25 prompts, and Mahalanobis kept picking Russian. He proposed a "density ratio" (a classifier between traffic and the mix) instead, but never ran it.

Behaviour

He fine-tuned models on made-up rules ("answer in rhyme", "hold a Catholic priest's moral views") to see whether the detector predicts where a rule fails to carry over. Rhyme carried over everywhere; for the priest rule the detector scored failures and successes the same (AUROC 0.51). On real ShareChat traffic judged by Gemini, label-free detectors were near the prompt-length baseline (0.51–0.59 against 0.575).

Side projects

Jailbreak detection did not beat proper baselines. Common backdoors were too easy to be a fair test.

Open questions he left

  • Do his numbers hold up when someone re-runs them?
  • Does the detector read the topic (what the prompt is about), or just its words, writing style or length?
  • How can prompt length be removed from rankings cleanly?
  • On real traffic, can the method find several distinct, nameable gaps, and can the clustering and naming be trusted?
  • How should an iterative search stop re-finding the same gap? Does his density-ratio idea work?
  • What about traffic he never tested on the 7B model: multi-turn conversations and tool use?
  • Do the gaps matter, meaning does the model behave worse on them?

3 · The setup shared by our experiments

Unless an experiment says otherwise, it uses the following. Each experiment section only lists what differs.

Model

allenai/Olmo-3-7B-Instruct-DPO, revision b33130b7, in bf16 precision on one GPU.

The mix

Dolci-Instruct-DPO conversations with exactly one user message and one reply; the user message is the prompt. Sampled in proportion to each of 25 source families: 3,000 prompts to fit the detectors (idfit) and a separate 1,000 held out (idmix).

Pre-training sample

3,000 Dolma v1.6 documents (2,603 web crawl, 212 C4, 120 Reddit, 60 academic papers, 5 books), first 512 tokens each.

Uncovered pools

Legal: 500 r/legaladvice posts, title plus body (jonathanli/legal-advice-reddit). Medical: 500 patient questions (lavita/ChatDoctor-HealthCareMagic-100k). The mix has no legal or medical family.

Covered comparison

500 English WildChat first prompts. Anything already in the mix was removed: 126 of 626 checked were members. A membership check matches exact text or a shared 13-word run, and we confirmed it catches 200 of 200 known mix prompts.

Features

Each prompt is put in the model's chat template, and only the user's own tokens are pooled (not the template). Layers 0 to 32 in steps of 4. Default setting: mean pooling at layer 16.

Length bands

exp01–exp03: 1–16, 16–32, 32–64, 64–128, 128–256, 256–512 and 512–1024 tokens. From exp04: bands four times narrower (each about 19% wider than the last).

exp01 · 18 Oct

Replicate Stefan's main claim

Question

On the Olmo-3 7B DPO model, against English WildChat at matched length, do one-sided distances fall to chance while two-sided distances stay high, as Stefan reported (about 0.50 vs 0.92–0.97)? And as a check that the pipeline works: do all detectors separate legal and medical from held-out mix prompts at 0.95 or more?

Method

The shared setup. Detectors: Mahalanobis (with Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage, which stabilises the estimate when there are fewer prompts than dimensions), kNN with k = 10, their two-sided versions, the tf-idf word counter, and prompt length. One forward pass over 16,000 prompts. 95% intervals from 1,000 bootstrap resamples.

One-sided Mahalanobis fails the easy case (0.685 for legal). It overfits: the median distance is 2,471 for the 3,000 prompts it was fitted on but 12,893 for held-out prompts from the same mix, so it tells fitted from held-out mix prompts at 0.985. With 4,096 dimensions and 3,000 prompts, the shape estimate is close to singular. Within every pool, Mahalanobis also tracks length (Spearman correlation −0.70 to −0.93): averaging more tokens pulls a prompt toward the centre.

Confounds

The word counter matches the two-sided detectors, so the signal could be vocabulary. Legal and medical come from Reddit and a medical Q&A site, WildChat from a chatbot, so source and writing style differ along with topic.

exp02 · 18 Oct

Try to fix Mahalanobis by reducing dimensions

Question

exp01's Mahalanobis overfits. If it is fitted in fewer dimensions, does it stop overfitting and pass the easy case?

Method

exp01's features. Each Mahalanobis is fitted in the top 256 principal components of its own reference set. Also tried 32 to 1,024 components, and fixed shrinkage of 0.1 and 0.5.

Results

The overfitting goes (held-out vs fitted mix 0.42–0.46) but accuracy drops. Easy case: one-sided 0.501 / 0.748 (exp01: 0.685 / 0.820). Hard case, two-sided Mahalanobis: 0.760 / 0.400 (exp01: 0.954 / 0.897). For legal, accuracy rises steadily with dimensions: 0.795 at 32, 0.869 at 512, 0.911 at 1,024, 0.954 at full rank. Fixed shrinkage behaves like exp01 (0.954 / 0.892 at 0.1).

So the useful signal sits in low-variance directions, which PCA throws away. We kept full-rank Mahalanobis.

Confounds

None specific beyond exp01's.

exp04 · 18 Oct

exp03 again, with measured coverage

Question

With exp03's three flaws fixed, does its pattern hold, and do detector scores follow how well the mix actually covers each post?

Method

Length bands four times narrower. Two fresh samples drawn evenly across the whole 2019 file (seeds 1 and 2), plus exp03's sample. Coverage measured for each post as the cosine similarity of its all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence embedding to the nearest of 233,642 unique Dolci prompts. Then the correlation between detector score and coverage across 4,000 posts, with length held fixed (partial Spearman).

Results

Same ordering on every sample. Median legal-vs-subreddit AUROC 0.704 / 0.707 / 0.686 (Mahalanobis − Dolma) and 0.610 / 0.632 / 0.621 (kNN − Dolma). Measured coverage varies little: subreddit medians run from 0.49 to 0.57, and legal is the least covered (0.495). The score-vs-coverage correlation is −0.16 to −0.20 (better-covered posts score slightly lower). The word counter gives −0.16 to −0.18.

Confounds

The coverage measure has a narrow range, so a weak correlation could be the measure's fault. Detectors and word counter look alike again.

exp05 · 18 Oct

Rewrite legal and medical prompts in the mix's style

Question

If legal and medical prompts are rewritten to read like mix prompts, keeping the same request, do the activation detectors still separate them from WildChat rewritten the same way, and do they beat the word counter?

Method

openai/gpt-4.1 rewrote 500 each of legal, medical, WildChat and held-out mix prompts. It was shown 8 example mix prompts and told to match their tone, structure and length while keeping the request and every fact. A judge (anthropic/claude-sonnet-4.6) confirmed each rewrite kept the request: 99%, 99%, 93% and 92% of rows.

Results

Legal rewrites vs WildChat rewrites: Mahalanobis − Dolma 0.959, kNN − Dolma 0.921, word counter 0.966. Medical: 0.945, 0.923, 0.904. The word counter's legal score went up from 0.951. The rewrites kept the topic words: one legal rewrite still says "campus police", "legal" and "prescribed". Rewriting also moved WildChat toward the mix more than it moved legal and medical.

Confounds

Because the topic words survived, a detector reading topic and one reading words would both still work. The test could not separate the two, so it says nothing either way about the detectors.

exp06 · 18 Oct

A traffic-vs-mix classifier on hidden prompts

Question

Stefan's untested idea: instead of comparing with Dolma, train a classifier to tell real traffic from the mix (a "density ratio") and rank traffic by it. Does it surface rare uncovered prompts hidden in real traffic, while leaving rare covered-but-unusual ones alone?

Method

10,000 WildChat first prompts, all languages, with mix members removed. Hidden in it: legal and medical (uncovered), plus multilingual (Aya) and code (Magicoder) prompts as covered controls, at 0.1%, 0.3% or 1% each, three random seeds. Classifier: regularised logistic regression on mean-pooled layer-16 activations, "real traffic" vs "mix". Each prompt is scored by a classifier trained without it (5 folds). Ranking within 20 equal-size length bins. Measure: hidden prompts in the top 100, as a multiple of random.

Results
RankingHidden at 0.3%Hidden at 1%
Traffic-vs-mix classifier0.0×1.39×
kNN − Dolma8.43×4.85×
One-sided Mahalanobis or kNN0.0×0.0×
Random0.56×0.69×

The classifier tells traffic from the mix well (AUROC 0.951), but its top 15 prompts are all WildChat, mostly in Chinese. kNN − Dolma lets few covered controls in (0.56× and 0.17×).

Confounds

About a third of the WildChat pool is Chinese, while the mix's non-English chat family is under 3% of its prompts. The classifier spends its top ranks on that one large difference before reaching any 1% topic.

exp07 · 18 Oct

The same, with English prompts only

Question

With language removed, does the classifier find the hidden legal and medical prompts?

Method

exp06 exactly, except the pool is 10,000 English WildChat prompts.

Results

Classifier 0.0× at 0.3% and 1.21× at 1% (random 0.56× / 0.69×). Its top 15 are now fan fiction and "what if" crossovers (Batman, SCP, Sonic, Half-Life). kNN − Dolma 3.37× / 2.77×, with no covered controls in its top 100.

Confounds

The classifier ranks the next-largest natural difference first. That makes it a tool for finding the biggest gaps, but a 1% hidden topic cannot compete with a natural gap of thousands of prompts.

exp08 · 18 Oct

Sanity checks exp01 never had

Question

Does the two-sided detector behave as a coverage detector should on Stefan's own checks?

Method and results
  • Seed-pair null. Use a second Dolma sample in place of the mix: "Dolma A minus Dolma B" should not separate legal from WildChat (about 0.5). Mahalanobis: 0.710 legal, 0.479 medical. kNN: 0.513, 0.535. Mahalanobis picks up a difference between two samples of the same thing, so kNN − Dolma became the detector used for gap search.
  • Membership. Prompts the model trained on vs unseen prompts from the same source (Aya, code, jailbreak; 500 each): 0.48–0.52. The detector does not react to "seen this exact prompt".
  • Same topic, other source. Code from Magicoder and multilingual prompts from Bactrian-X, sources the mix never used, against the mix's own code and Aya prompts: 0.36–0.57. The detector treats them as covered.
  • A direction that never sees the mix (WildChat towards Dolma): legal vs WildChat 0.781 at layer 16 and 0.971 at layer 32. Part of the legal signal is "Reddit text looks like pre-training text".
  • Supervised probes on exp05's rewrites: both the activation probe and the word probe score above 0.997, so they could not be compared.

Also: perplexity alone separates legal and medical from WildChat at 0.892 / 0.776 (length-controlled). In real traffic, two-sided Mahalanobis correlates with length at 0.90–0.93; kNN at 0.43–0.48.

Confounds

The word counter also passes the membership and other-source checks (0.47–0.51 and 0.33–0.57). Passing them shows the detector is not broken, not that it beats counting words.

exp09 · 19 Oct

First search for gaps in real traffic

Question

Stefan's top priority. Comparing the real mix with real traffic, can an iterative search find several distinct, nameable gaps, more than reviewing random prompts finds?

Method

Pool: 20,000 real first prompts, all languages, mix members removed: exp06's 10,000 WildChat plus 10,000 LMSYS. Two rankings, both within length bins: the traffic-vs-mix classifier, and kNN − Dolma. Five rounds, each doing:

  • Take the 500 top-ranked prompts.
  • Embed them with a multilingual sentence model (paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2) and split them into 10 clusters (k-means).
  • Show 25 prompts from each cluster to claude-sonnet-4.6, with no mention of the project. It replies with a name, a description, and COHERENT yes/no ("do most of these clearly share a topic, task, format or language?").
  • A cluster counts as a gap if it is coherent, has 15 or more prompts, and its median coverage is below 0.600, the pool's 25th percentile.
  • Confirmed gaps are added to the mix side so the next round looks elsewhere.

The same steps run on 500 random prompts each round, as the comparison.

Results

Gaps confirmed, ranked vs random: classifier 0 vs 1; kNN − Dolma 3 vs 1. The three were LMSYS prompts with NAME_1 placeholders, mostly explicit roleplay (50 prompts); short technical error messages (40); and LLM-evaluation templates (18). The namer called 12–13 of 50 clusters coherent in every group, ranked and random alike. 19 of 50 kNN clusters fell below the coverage cut, against 1 of 50 random. Coherent clusters that missed the cut include the Chinese chemical-company advertising cluster from Stefan's notes (coverage 0.667–0.681) and WildChat "script about a college football upset" prompts (0.614).

Confounds

Found afterwards: only confirmed clusters left the pool, so rejected prompts were ranked and reviewed again in every round. The classifier, which confirmed nothing, reviewed the same 500 five times, while the random comparison saw 500 new prompts each round. The two did not get the same review budget.

exp10 · 19 Oct

Rewrite again, banning the giveaway words

Question

exp05's fix: if the rewriter may not use the words a word counter relies on, does the word counter fall toward 0.5 while the activation detectors still flag legal and medical?

Method

Ban list: train word counters to tell legal from WildChat and medical from WildChat on separate data, and take the 40 strongest words in each direction, 225 words in total. gpt-4.1 rewrote the 500 legal, medical and WildChat prompts without them. Any rewrite that still used a banned word got one repair call. A blind judge then checked the topic survived (LAWYER, DOCTOR or NEITHER). The pool prompts were re-extracted, and exp01's numbers reproduced (0.9544 / 0.8972).

Results

After repair, 83% of legal and 66% of medical rewrites still contained a banned word (97% and 94% before repair), mostly everyday words such as "told", "know" and "weeks". The judge still saw the topic in 93.6% and 99.2%. Rewrites vs WildChat rewrites: word counter 0.952 / 0.837, Mahalanobis − Dolma 0.934 / 0.944, kNN − Dolma 0.917 / 0.923. On medical the activation detectors beat the word counter by 0.107 and 0.087; on legal they trail it by 0.02–0.04. Probes trained on the original prompts score the rewrites at 1.000 (activations), 0.987 (words) and 0.998 (a sentence-embedding model).

Confounds

The rewriter could not follow a 225-word ban, so the words were still there and the word counter still worked. Medical rewrites and WildChat rewrites differ in length enough that length alone scores 0.560 within bands.

exp11 · 19 Oct

Which ranking finds hidden gaps

Question

Stefan's suggested fixes for length and for the search loop, none of them tested before: do any surface hidden gaps better than ranking within length bins with mean pooling?

Method

exp09's 20,000-prompt pool with 100 legal and 100 medical (the gap, 1% together) and 100 multilingual and 100 code (covered) hidden in it, three seeds. Loop variants, with a reviewer that knows the true labels, over 5 rounds of 200: remove reviewed prompts only; add confirmed ones to the mix (exp09's rule); or drop prompts close to confirmed ones.

exp12 · 19 Oct

A second, smaller model

Question

Does exp01's pattern hold on a different model and mix? Stefan reported that on his second pair, two-sided did not beat one-sided.

Method

allenai/OLMo-2-0425-1B-SFT, with its post-training data allenai/tulu-3-sft-olmo-2-mixture-0225 (866,128 conversations, 19 sources) as the mix: 3,000 fit and 1,000 held out, by source share. Layer 8 of 16. Same legal, medical and Dolma files. 500 English WildChat prompts with Tulu members removed (369 of 869).

Results

Length-controlled vs WildChat, legal / medical: one-sided Mahalanobis 0.684 / 0.884, one-sided kNN 0.622 / 0.741, Mahalanobis − Dolma 0.941 / 0.919, kNN − Dolma 0.926 / 0.922, word counter 0.918 / 0.824, length 0.51 / 0.47. exp13 · 19 Oct

Multi-turn conversations and tool use

Question

Two kinds of traffic never tested on the 7B model. Is a user's second message in a real conversation a gap, with and without the earlier turns as context? Are tool-calling prompts a gap? And does the mix's own small multi-turn data read as covered?

Method

3.85% of Dolci-DPO is multi-turn, all of it in eight synthetic families, and none of it is tool use. Pools of 500: real WildChat second messages, scored with the first exchange in front (context) and alone, with the same pooled tokens either way; held-out Dolci multi-turn rows; and Hermes function-calling prompts with and without their tool list. Reference: the 3,000 mix prompts plus 500 Dolci multi-turn rows.

Results

Against first-message WildChat, kNN − Dolma: Dolci's own multi-turn rows 0.167 (read as covered); real second messages 0.697 with context and 0.523 alone, a paired difference of +0.175. Without the 500 multi-turn rows in the reference, the context version reaches 0.853. Tool-use prompts are long (median 328 tokens against 57), so length alone scores 0.835 even within bands and they cannot be compared fairly; their kNN − Dolma score is 0.773.

Confounds

The earlier turns include ChatGPT's own replies, so part of the context effect may be the assistant's writing style rather than the conversation itself. Run by the other agent session.

exp14 · 19 Oct

Checking the gap-confirmation step on known answers

Question

exp09 rests on three steps nobody had checked: clustering, the LLM's coherent call, and the coverage cut. When we know the answer, do they confirm uncovered topics and reject covered ones?

Method

exp09's pool, namer, embedding model and coverage cut, plus 300-prompt banks: legal and medical (uncovered), code and multilingual (covered). Three seeds. No detector ranking is involved.

  • Namer test: hand-built 50-prompt clusters that are 100%, 70% or 30% one topic (the rest random traffic), or fully random. Named blind; a second model (openai/gpt-4.1) maps each name to LEGAL, MEDICAL, CODE or OTHER.
  • Coverage test: median coverage of the 100% clusters against the 0.600 cut.
  • End to end: a 500-prompt batch of 400 random traffic prompts plus 25 of each topic goes through the full step (10 clusters, naming, the gap rule).
Results

Namer: coherent for 9 of 9 pure clusters and 0 of 9 random ones, but also 0 of 9 clusters that are 70% one topic, which it names "Miscellaneous" or "Diverse" queries. Its names match the topic for 9 of 9 pure clusters and 3 of 9 70% clusters. The same check with a counting namer

Question

exp14's namer rejects clusters that are 70% one topic. If it counts instead, does the step then confirm legal too, without confirming covered topics?

Method

exp14's 69 clusters, checked to be identical in every prompt. One change: the namer is asked to find the largest group of the shown prompts sharing one topic, task, format or language, name it, and say how many of the 25 belong. A cluster is coherent if that count is at least half.

4 · Where things stand

Replication

Stefan's two-sided result reproduces on both models (0.92–0.95 against real WildChat at matched length). His "one-sided falls to chance" does not hold on the smaller model (0.62–0.88).

Topic or words

Open. The word counter is within a few hundredths of the activation detectors almost everywhere. Both attempts to remove the words while keeping the topic (exp05, exp10) failed to remove the words.

Length

Ranking within length bins is the only method so far that balances length. Within those bins, max pooling (7.5×) and whole-cluster review (30×) find hidden gaps, where single-prompt mean pooling finds none (exp11).

Real gaps

One search in real traffic, with the older recipe, found three gaps (one an anonymisation artifact, two the same tech topic) and none for random review. The improved recipe (max pooling, whole clusters, removing only reviewed prompts, a namer that accepts mixed clusters) has not been run on real traffic yet.

The naming step

On known answers, clustering and the coverage measure work, and the step has never confirmed a covered topic. The namer is the weak part: one version rejects mixed clusters, the other splits pure ones into narrow subtopics. No person has labelled any real cluster.

Traffic types

The mix's synthetic multi-turn data reads as covered. Real second messages with context are partly flagged (0.70). Tool use could not be compared fairly because of length.

Behaviour

Not tested: no one has checked whether the model behaves worse on any flagged prompt.

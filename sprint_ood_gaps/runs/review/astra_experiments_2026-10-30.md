Read-only, offline audit. I checked the producing scripts and selected saved inputs for sampling, overlap and denominator problems; I did not repeat the previous numerical reproduction.

1. **Slide 5 — topic split: CHANGES-CLAIM.**  
   Trace: randomly omit topics’ A halves → score B halves against retained A halves → topic-median AUROC measures recovery of synthetic cluster membership. The reference is **traffic**, not Dolci; “training-only” should mean reference-only here. Word-derived topics also give words a home advantage. The main “no resolved improvement” wording is defensible **on this partition**, not as detector equivalence. Secondary grouped results have dev/test contamination at the group level: I found shared groups in 178/200 LSA groups and 185/200 MiniLM groups. Assignment is per group, but dev/test is per topic. The combination also computes percentile ranks across dev and test together—unlabelled transductive calibration, not test-label leakage. [Split construction](scripts/topic_split_test.py:24), [reference](scripts/topic_split_test.py:50), [combination](scripts/detector_improve_analyse.py:16).  
   **Supports:** these scorers discriminate omitted versus retained clusters under the tested partitions; the slide mostly respects that limit.

2. **Slide 5 — family hold-out: CHANGES-CLAIM.**  
   Trace: remove named source families and query hashes → compare removed-family prompts with held-out kept-source prompts → measures source-family distinctness. Removing the listed code sources does not establish that coding is absent elsewhere. Exact-copy exclusion is implemented, but templates and near-copies are not held out as families; negatives can retain their source’s templates. The six test families, rather than thousands of prompts, govern generalisation; the displayed family interval reflects that small unit count. “Beat random and length on our benchmarks” is too categorical when the post-hoc best family result’s interval includes chance. [Families and sampling](scripts/one_sided_score.py:13), [reference exclusion](scripts/one_sided_score.py:76), [family bootstrap](scripts/examples_detectors_slides_3_6.py:404).  
   **Supports:** modest, heterogeneous source-family discrimination, not reliable detection of genuinely missing capabilities.

3. **Slide 6 — removal_3b: CHANGES-CLAIM; numerical approximation also needs disclosure.**  
   Trace: remove source families or language-ID-labelled non-English rows → compare before/after distances → AUROC measures selective response to deletion. “Take every Dolci prompt of one kind out” overstates the safety/code removals: these use source lists. TF-IDF is fitted on **full Dolci, including removed rows**, so this is deletion from a frozen representation, not fitting on the remaining reference alone. “Recompute each traffic prompt’s distance to the rest” is also inexact: when all 500 cached neighbours disappear, the code substitutes the 500th original distance. Topic outcomes are differences of medians; language outcomes are prompt-level and share the language identifier used for removal. [Removal definitions](scripts/removal_3b.py:38), [distance fallback](scripts/removal_3b.py:48), [TF-IDF fitting](scripts/removal_3b.py:62), [metrics](scripts/removal_3b.py:90).  
   **Supports:** both frozen scorers respond selectively to these deletions beyond the sampled random controls; this does not establish successful gap-list recovery.

4. **Slide 7 — unprimed relabel: MINOR.**  
   Trace: show ten topic members without the name → explicitly prompt ten axes → classify the defining axis. This is **name-blind, axis-prompted** labelling, not unprimed discovery. “Read fresh” does not mean disjoint evidence: reconstructing the original namer’s sampling showed 70 reused prompts across 37/56 confirmed topics. The 79% name statistic also covers all reviewed topic-name pairs, while the 36%/38% comparison covers confirmed topics. [Prompt and sampling](scripts/gap_taxonomy_label.py:11), [sampling pool](scripts/gap_taxonomy_label.py:64), [different denominators](scripts/gap_taxonomy_analyse.py:19).  
   **Supports:** this rubric often assigns confirmed topics a template/placeholder axis despite subject-like names; it does not isolate naming bias from sampling and rubric effects.

5. **Slide 7 — one-line rules: MINOR.**  
   Trace: measure three properties among non-member topic prompts → flag topics with a majority matching any rule → count overlap with confirmed/rejected/random sets. This measures overlap with the loop, not heuristic precision or equivalent discovery performance. The rules were selected after the taxonomy results exposed these artefacts; fixing the 50% cutoff before counting does not make their selection non-exploratory. The random set excludes confirmed topics, and the template rule uses traffic-wide prefix frequencies. [Implementation](scripts/gap_heuristic.py:12), [selection rationale](designs/gap_taxonomy.md:139).  
   **Supports:** three retrospectively motivated rules flag many confirmed topics; mark this comparison exploratory.

6. **Slide 7 — LLM precision, part 18: MINOR for the narrowed slide wording.**  
   Trace: sample eligible loop gaps and below-line controls → show ten non-Dolci-member prompts with four truncated retrieved neighbours → judge screen coverage. This is not precision against all Dolci: exact members are excluded, neighbours are cut to 300 characters, and the controls are below-line topics rather than unrestricted random topics. Retrieval failure and selection for distance remain coupled. “Below the pre-set 0.5 bar” describes the point-estimate decision rule; it does not establish precision below 0.5 statistically. [Selection and retrieval](scripts/loop_labels_build.py:16), [truncation](scripts/loop_labels_build.py:49), [judgement rule](scripts/loop_labels_llm.py:13).  
   **Supports:** the reported proportion fails this particular screen-based point-estimate bar. Also, the design/report’s self-selection concern misidentifies the original rater: the saved run used Gemini, not Sonnet. [Run configuration](runs/loop_onesided/v2/config.json:15).

7. **Slide 8 — property tags, part 8: CHANGES-NUMBER.**  
   Trace: sample scored queries → tag properties → estimate prevalence and distance AUROC. The sampling does **not** yield near-uniform traffic. It excludes the 26.5% of indexed traffic found verbatim in Dolci, then draws without replacement from at most 25 queries per topic, weighted by total topic size. The largest topic is 6.9% of traffic but contributes only 25/2,000 = 1.25% of the sample. Neither prevalence nor AUROC analysis corrects this sampling. Thus “Traffic vs Dolci” percentages are sample proportions, not estimates for the claimed traffic population. Wilson intervals also omit topic/template and sampling-design dependence. Finally, clipping at 2,000 characters can itself create the “cut off mid-text” property being measured. [Sampling](scripts/gap_facets.py:57), [clipping](scripts/gap_facets.py:77), [unweighted analysis](scripts/gap_facets.py:130).  
   **Supports:** these properties differ in this selected sample and associate weakly or negatively with distance there; the population claims require changing.

8. **Slide 9 — fixed-property null, review_part15_null20: CHANGES-CLAIM.**  
   Trace: retain known tags → rank traffic-minus-Dolci prevalence → compare target-removal ranks with random controls. The null removes only **other families**, preserving every target-family prompt; it is not unrestricted random deletion and can mechanically increase the target family’s remaining share. The tags and target mappings already encode the removals. Their success criterion—best-rank improvement—is weaker than the induced-method requirement of two extra top-ten descriptions. The slide discloses this, but its headline still treats the outcomes as comparable. [Target mappings and ranking](scripts/review_part15_null20.py:23), [restricted null](scripts/review_part15_null20.py:33).  
   **Supports:** selected existing tags move predictably after targeted source removal; it does not establish an advantage of fixed taxonomies over induced ones.

9. **Slide 9 — grouping recovery, part 16: CHANGES-CLAIM.**  
   Trace: freeze two groupings of the full mixed sample → rank group prevalence differences → require both +2 target groups and a best rank beating 19/20 controls. This tests that conjunction, not the headline “do not rank it above a random removal.” “0 of 12 grouping settings” means two groupings × two filtering conditions × three removals, all reusing one sample and one clustering seed—not twelve independent attempts. The category names themselves are judged from sampled descriptions. [Grouping construction](scripts/gap_emergent.py:79), [ranking and success rule](scripts/gap_grouping_recovery.py:26).  
   **Supports:** neither grouping clears the chosen incremental-recovery rule; the exploratory change-based check shows that the groupings nevertheless contain removal information. [Change-based check](scripts/gap_grouping_visible.py:18).

10. **Slide 9 — published methods and judge, part 12: CHANGES-CLAIM, plus CHANGES-NUMBER for the displayed denominator.**  
    Trace: generate/tag concepts → rank source differences → judge description text → count target descriptions relative to one random arm. The figure’s **“top 10 (of 10)” is false**: saved lists contain 3–8 HypotheSAEs descriptions, 4–5 LLooM descriptions, and two seeded-LLooM descriptions per arm. HypotheSAEs first selects 20 features by **absolute** source separation, then the wrapper discards negative features; LLooM also discards nonpositive differences. The +2 bar consequently has very different opportunities across methods. [HypotheSAEs filtering](scripts/gap_hypothesaes.py:55), [absolute selection](.venv/lib/python3.12/site-packages/hypothesaes/select_neurons.py:181), [LLooM filtering](scripts/gap_lloom.py:104), [incorrect caption](scripts/gap_recovery_fig.py:22).  
    LLooM concepts are induced once before removal; InsTag counts lexically normalised tags without semantic deduplication; description counts therefore also measure granularity. The judge rejudges identical descriptions across arms—I found one inconsistent Sonnet description, none for Astra. [InsTag normalisation](scripts/gap_instag_analyse.py:9), [judge construction](scripts/gap_removal_judge.py:37).  
    **Supports:** these particular pipelines did not gain two judged descriptions against their single controls; the headline claims more.

11. **Slide 9 — NBER, part 21: CHANGES-CLAIM.**

Safety and multilingual have empty eligible-category lists in code, so failure of the named-category test is built in. That cannot independently establish that categories are necessary for detecting a removal. The max-drop statistic can detect an unnamed shift; the saved report says it passes for safety too, contradicting the speaker-note phrase **“the safety removal is invisible.”** The displayed `specific_info` rank is a post-hoc category-specific illustration, not the pre-specified named-category endpoint. [Empty target lists](scripts/gap_nber_topic_rank.py:17), [max-drop test](scripts/gap_nber_topic_rank.py:45), [selected plotting categories](scripts/gap_nber_topic_fig.py:12), [safety result](runs/gap_taxonomy/nber_topic/report.md:21).  
    **Supports:** programming’s fine category changes rank; coarse aggregation can hide it, and other removals can appear under different names.

12. **Slide 10 — NAME_k probe: MINOR.**

This measures the substitution’s effect on refusal in the probe. **“Real names put back” is inaccurate:** the script inserts eight fixed synthetic names; it does not restore original identities. Four replies are averaged before prompt-level analysis, rather than treated as four independent prompts. [Substitution](external/ood/scripts/run_build_placeholder_probe.py:18), [aggregation](scripts/fix_loop_analysis.py:19).

13. **Slide 10 — training run: MINOR for refusal reduction; causal interpretation remains unsupported.**  
    Trace: generate answers to renamed prompts → discard refusals → map names back → train fix/control adapters → compare held-out refusal. The training objective teaches answering, with no independent harmlessness requirement. Lower refusal therefore does not itself mean better answers. I found no exact normalised training/evaluation prompt overlap. The trainer uses reply-token loss and unloads adapters between arms; the analysis pairs prompts and retains the control adapter. [Training construction](scripts/fix_loop_build_train.py:11), [trainer](external/ood/scripts/run_train_lora.py:47), [comparison](scripts/fix_loop_analysis.py:24).  
    **Supports:** one corrective training recipe reduces measured refusal. The harmful-regression percentages come from a manual review aggregation, not this analysis script; the original grader labels harm per response with the response visible. [Grading](scripts/fix2_grade.py:23), [manual aggregation](runs/review/fix2_check_2026-10-24.md:5).

14. **Slide 10 — answer scan, part 14c: MINOR for the current narrow wording.**  
    Trace: identify keyword refusals → harm-grade a sample of refused prompts → test word enrichment against non-refused answers plus sampled “harmless” refusals. This measures enrichment in a selected, grader-defined outcome, not refusal probability among independently established harmless requests. Non-refused prompts receive no harm labels; missing/unsampled refusals are excluded. The grader explicitly exempts adult roleplay, while the later reading rubric treats much of it as intended refusal. Holm correction handles multiple words, not dependence between templated prompts. [Outcome construction](scripts/gap_answer_14c.py:77), [grader definition](external/ood/src/generation/request_grader.py:18).  
    **Supports:** the scan recovers NAME_k as a refusal-associated word under this instrument; the slide’s explicit “finds the token, not harmless prompts answered with refusals” is the appropriate scope.

15. **Slide 10 — answer scan/source check, part 14d: CHANGES-CLAIM.**  
    Trace: classify words from five excerpts, then compare NAME_k refusal within WildGuardMix → measures semantic cue judgements and within-source association. It does not control for request type within that heterogeneous source. Calling a word content-free is not evidence the model uses it causally. Word-level precision intervals treat correlated words as independent; the code acknowledges this. The underlying answer census retains only the first occurrence of each normalised prompt, so it also does not measure training exposure frequencies or all associated answers. [Word-level analysis](scripts/gap_answer_14d.py:40), [source comparison](scripts/gap_answer_14d.py:59), [answer deduplication](scripts/trig_label.py:25).

16. **Slide 11 — turn counts: CHANGES-CLAIM for the comparison’s units.**  
    Trace: count user-role messages → aggregate by first-turn text or stored conversation row. The 37.7% implementation takes the **maximum** turn count across every conversation matching a normalised first turn; its main bullet now states that correctly. But part 22’s **“deduplicated … conversations”** removes duplicates by first-user text alone and keeps the first occurrence, discarding distinct continuations. Dolci’s comparison is over all stored SFT rows. These are different units and deduplication rules, not matched conversation populations. [Maximum join](scripts/gap_taxonomy_blindspots.py:30), [first-text deduplication](scripts/gap_turn_counts.py:23), [Dolci denominator](scripts/gap_turn_counts.py:39).  
    **Supports:** later turns are omitted by this pipeline and multi-user-turn SFT rows outside Tool Use are scarce; the exact traffic/SFT contrast needs its asymmetric units stated. The 128-token count is descriptive. The second-embedder overlap uses the older **mean-top-ten** ranking, not the current nearest-one loop, and changes embedder and context window together. [Ranking](scripts/robust_analyse.py:14).

**(a) Least willing to defend:** part 12 as a comparison of method failure, because reporting budgets and success opportunities differ; part 8 as population prevalence, because the sampler demonstrably distorts topic mass; part 21 as confirmation of category necessity, because unnamed targets are ineligible by construction.

**(b) Slide 2 claims I would state:**

- “On our synthetic topic-reference benchmark, reference-only scorers discriminate omitted clusters above chance; we find no resolved gain over TF-IDF on word-derived clusters. Deletion responses differ by scorer.”
- “Recovery depends on the representation, category granularity and reporting rule. Existing target tags shift after deletion; our induced-category pipelines did not clear the chosen incremental-reporting threshold.”
- “Replacing NAME_k with fixed names reduces refusal on our probe, and one corrective training run reduces it further. Refused Dolci answers suggest a possible training-data mechanism; its causal contribution is unisolated.”

**(c) Drop:** the fixed-versus-induced success/failure headline; NBER as a confirmation that a named category is necessary; “so Olmo learned the token”; and the part-8 percentages presented as corpus prevalence. Drop “of 10” from the recovery figure unless actual list lengths are shown.

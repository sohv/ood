# What kinds of gap can the loop report? (written 2026-10-29 MT, before any label)

## Question
Callum (29 Oct): the OOD score is probably not the limiting part; "what counts as a gap" is. The loop can only report a gap as one MiniLM topic cluster, named by a namer prompted for a shared subject. Gaps that matter may be kinds of prompt rather than subjects: hidden difficulty, multi-turn, adversarial pressure, long context, templates. So: (1) what property actually defines each gap the loop reports; (2) how often the namer's name misses it; (3) which kinds of gap the setup cannot report at all; (4) does a property-first grouping of far-from-Dolci prompts find kinds the topic-first loop does not?

## Chain
1. Units = gaps the loop already produced, no new loop runs. Full one-sided run (`runs/loop_onesided/v2`, R50 confirmed) and every removal arm's new gaps (`runs/loop_removal/*`, R50 confirmed and not confirmed in the full run), plus all 200 reviewed topics of the full run as the candidate pool (confirmed and rejected). ✓ Answers (1)-(2) for what the loop reports and what its confirmation step drops.
2. Prompts shown per gap = 10 members drawn at random (seed 0) from `runs/removal_3b/queries.parquet` (25 non-member prompts per topic), not the namer's own examples. ✓ The namer saw its own sample; a fresh sample keeps the labeller from inheriting it.
3. Labeller prompt = open-ended, not primed for subject: "What do these prompts have in common?" Every shared property, each tagged with one axis from a fixed list (subject, task type, language, format or template, length or structure, persona or roleplay, adversarial or jailbreak, tone or register, placeholder or artefact, other), then the ONE property that best defines the group, and whether the given loop name describes that property. The loop name is shown only after the labeller has answered (two-turn prompt within one call: answer, then see name, then judge). ✓ Callum's point is that topic-primed labelling hides non-topic properties; an unprimed labeller is the measurement.
4. Labellers: Claude Sonnet (claude -p) on all units; codex Sole on a random 60 for agreement on the defining axis (Cohen's kappa). ✓ One labeller family can share the namer's bias; the second checks the axis call.
5. Measures: share of gaps by defining axis; share where the name misses the defining property; same for rejected vs confirmed topics (does the confirmation step drop non-subject groups?); same for safety-removal new gaps vs random-removal new gaps.
6. Blind spots (no labeller): counts over the 992,933 traffic prompts of properties the setup cannot see or report: 2+ user turns in the source conversation (first turn only is scored), longer than MiniLM's 128-token window, non-English mixed within topics, system-prompt or template prefixes, code blocks. ✓ These are structural limits; counting them needs no model.
7. Property-first grouping (part C): the 5% of traffic prompts farthest from Dolci by one-sided MiniLM kNN (prompt level, not topic level), 50 k-means clusters on them, same labeller and axes. Compare the axis distribution with step 5. ✓ Tells whether grouping by distance first and naming second surfaces non-subject kinds the topic-first loop cannot. Limit: still MiniLM space.

## What each result would mean
- Most defining properties are subject, names describe them: the taxonomy is topic-shaped and the loop reports subject gaps; the non-topic gaps Callum describes are out of its reach by construction (step 6 counts how much traffic that is).
- Many defining properties are template, language, artefact or adversarial and names miss them: the namer imposes topics; a property-first labeller would change the list.
- Confirmed and rejected differ by axis: the confirmation step filters by kind of property, not only by coverage.

## Budget
About 270 units x 1 Sonnet call + 60 Sole calls + 50 cluster calls; CPU only. Checkpoint 1: read the first 20 labels before running the rest. Checkpoint 2: tables. Checkpoint 3: part C vs part A.

## Part 8 (written 2026-10-29 MT before any tag): gaps as prompt properties, not clusters
Callum: a "topic" can be any yes/no property of a prompt (multi-turn, adversarial, long context, buried lead). Question: which prompt properties are common in real traffic and rare in Dolci, and does MiniLM distance to Dolci rank prompts with those properties high?
1. Samples: 2,000 traffic first turns drawn from step 3's 49,629 non-member queries (`runs/removal_3b/queries.parquet`) with probability proportional to topic size / 25, so the sample is close to uniform over traffic and every prompt already has its MiniLM k = 1 distance to full Dolci; 2,000 Dolci prompts uniform over the 1,710,578 (seed 0). ✓ Prevalence needs near-uniform samples; reusing scored queries links each tag to the detector without a new kNN pass.
2. Tagger: Claude Sonnet 5.5, 20 prompts per call, traffic and Dolci shuffled together and source not shown; each prompt up to 2,000 characters (beyond MiniLM's 128 tokens and the namer's 300 characters). Fixed schema: task (one of 14) and 16 yes/no facets, including Callum's (adversarial pressure, hidden difficulty, false premise, needs missing context, pasted input, roleplay or persona setup, system-prompt-like rules) and artefacts (placeholder or truncation, templated dataset item). ✓ Per-prompt tags remove the cluster and the namer from the measurement.
3. Measures: per facet, share in traffic vs Dolci (Wilson 95% intervals) and the ratio; within traffic, AUROC of MiniLM distance for prompts with vs without the facet (0.5 = distance does not see it).
4. Agreement: codex Sole tags the same schema on 200 of the prompts (10 calls of 20, seed 3); per-facet Cohen's kappa. Facets with kappa < 0.4 are reported as unreliable, not interpreted.
Size basis: 2,000 per side gives a share near 5% an interval of about plus or minus 1 point; a facet at 1% vs 4% is resolved. About 200 Sonnet calls + 10 Sole calls.

## Part 9: contrastive, verified properties (research-6d; written 2026-10-29 MT before any call)
Question: when the labeller sees a group next to its nearest Dolci prompts, what does the group have that Dolci lacks, and does that property hold up when checked blind? (Callum: what kinds of gap; propose-then-verify as in Zhong et al. 2022, which is not in our checked reviews.)
Chain:
1. Units: part 7's 50 far clusters; the full one-sided run's 56 confirmed R50 topics (`runs/loop_onesided/v2`); control = part 7's 50 random-traffic clusters (does the procedure find verifiable differences for any group, not only far ones?). ✓ Same units as parts 2 and 7, plus a control.
2. Members: traffic prompts not verbatim in Dolci. Per unit, 30 drawn at random (seed 0): 10 shown, 20 held out (fewer if the unit has fewer than 30; reported). Nearest Dolci prompt for each = MiniLM cosine top-1 over all 1,710,578 Dolci prompts, exact copies excluded. ✓ The same neighbour the loop's rater sees.
3. Propose (one Sonnet 5.5 call per unit, claude -p, research-d4's call settings): the 10 shown members, each paired with its nearest Dolci prompt (labelled A and B, 600 characters each). "What do the A messages have that the B messages lack? State ONE property as a yes/no question that can be answered about a single message, plus its axis from the part 3 list." ✓ Contrast with the training side instead of describing the group alone.
4. Verify (one Sonnet call per unit, blind): the predicate applied to 40 messages in random order with source hidden: the 20 held-out members and the 20 nearest-Dolci prompts of those held-out members. Measures per unit: member rate (share of members where the answer is yes) and Dolci rate. ✓ Held-out members, and neighbours of the held-out members, so the predicate is tested on prompts it was not written from.
5. Pass rule (a guess, no earlier basis; fixed now): member rate >= 0.5 and member rate minus Dolci rate >= 0.4. Report the full distribution as well, so the threshold is visible.
6. Measures: per set (far, confirmed, random), share of units that pass; axis tally of passing predicates; list of passing predicates. Most likely objection: the same model family proposes and verifies, so a predicate can pass because the verifier reads it the way the proposer meant; the random control shows how often that happens for groups that are not far from Dolci.
Budget: 156 propose + 156 verify Sonnet calls, at most 6 at once (shared subscription); CPU for neighbours. Checkpoint: read the first 10 proposals and their verifications before reading tables.
Amendment (before any tag): the facet list is widened to 34 to cover every kind Callum named on the call (difficult advice, attachments, long pasted context, buried request, tests of the model, agentic use, tone, red-teaming) and more (high-stakes domains, crisis, deception, privacy, current info, homework, copied text, strict format, conflicting instructions). The tagger returns the names of facets that apply plus a free-text "other_notable" per prompt, so properties not on the list can surface; those phrases are grouped after tagging (exploratory).

## Part 10: an emergent taxonomy from request descriptions (research-name; written 2026-10-29 MT before any call)
Question: if prompts are grouped by an LLM's description of what kind of request each one is (not its subject), and the categories come from the data rather than a fixed list (part 8) or a contrast with Dolci (part 9), which kinds are common in traffic and rare in Dolci? Does describing first change which groups separate traffic from Dolci, compared with grouping the raw text? Do the loop's confirmed gaps fall in those kinds?
Chain:
1. Sample = part 8's `runs/gap_taxonomy/facets/sample.jsonl` (2,000 traffic close to uniform over traffic, 2,000 Dolci uniform). ✓ Prevalence needs near-uniform samples; the same prompts as part 8 let the two be compared prompt by prompt.
2. Describe (Clio-style facet): Claude Sonnet 5.5 (G.CMD), 20 prompts per call in the sample's order (traffic and Dolci already shuffled, source not shown), up to 2,000 characters each. Asked for one or two sentences per prompt on what kind of request it is: the task, the form (language, length, structure, fixed template wording, pasted material, persona or scenario setup, references to content not included), and anything that would make it hard or unusual to answer well. Asked to leave out the subject matter and any names. No list of categories is given. ✓ The categories come from free text. Hiding the subject is the point of Callum's steer: kinds of prompt, not subjects.
3. Embed descriptions with paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2 (the loop's embedder), unit-normalised. ✓ The baseline (step 7) uses the same embedder, so the only difference between the two arms is the description step.
4. Fine level: k-means with k = 100 (sklearn, n_init 10, seed 0), about 40 prompts per node. Coarse level: Ward agglomerative clustering of the 100 fine centroids into 20. ✓ Clio clusters with k-means, then merges. 40 per node gives a traffic-share interval of about ±0.15 at 0.5, so a 0.9-traffic node separates from 0.5. Merging is done on centroids, not with Clio's LLM-proposed hierarchy, because that is the simplest version.
5. Names: one Sonnet call per node (100 fine + 20 coarse). Each call gets 20 random member descriptions (all of them if fewer), source hidden, and is asked for a short name for the kind of request plus one sentence. ✓ Naming from descriptions, not prompts, keeps the namer off subject. The source is hidden so the name cannot say "real users".
6. Measures per node: traffic count t, Dolci count d; traffic share t/(t+d) with a Wilson 95% interval; prevalence ratio t/d; median MiniLM distance (k = 1, to full Dolci) of its traffic members against the median of the 2,000. Traffic-heavy node (fixed now; the thresholds are a guess with no earlier basis): t >= 20 (at least 1% of traffic) and the Wilson lower bound of the traffic share >= 0.75 (about 3:1). Dolci-heavy is defined the same way in the other direction, for context. Per traffic-heavy node: 5 random traffic members (seed 0) with the node name, written to a markdown file. No examples are chosen by hand. The full ranked table is reported, so the thresholds are visible.
7. Baseline: steps 3-6 on MiniLM embeddings of the raw text of the same 4,000 prompts (128 tokens, as in the loop). The baseline nodes are named from the same member descriptions, so names are comparable and only the grouping differs. Comparison: (a) adjusted mutual information between node and source, fine and coarse; the number of traffic-heavy nodes and the share of traffic they hold; (b) overlap: the share of traffic prompts in description-arm traffic-heavy nodes that are also in baseline traffic-heavy nodes, and the reverse; the adjusted Rand index between the two arms.
8. Stability (most likely objection: the nodes are description noise or one k-means seed). (a) k-means seeds 1 and 2: for each seed-0 traffic-heavy node, the best Jaccard match at the other seed and whether that match is also traffic-heavy. (b) Re-description: 200 random prompts (seed 4) described again in a different batch order (10 calls). Each new description is assigned to the nearest seed-0 fine centroid; report the share that lands in the same fine node and in the same coarse node.
9. Map-back: the 56 confirmed topics of the full one-sided run (`runs/loop_onesided/v2/reviewed_topics_R50.jsonl`), 10 prompts each from parts 1-5 (`runs/gap_taxonomy/units.jsonl`, 560 prompts). Control: 56 of the 143 rejected topics drawn at random (seed 0), 560 prompts. These are described in calls of 20 with confirmed and rejected shuffled together and the topic not shown. Each prompt is assigned to the nearest fine centroid (description arm), and its raw text to the nearest raw centroid (baseline). Measures: the share of prompts in traffic-heavy nodes for confirmed, rejected and the 2,000-prompt traffic sample, with Wilson intervals; per confirmed topic, the node most of its prompts land in. ✓ The 2,000 sample holds only 43 prompts from confirmed topics, too few, so their prompts are described fresh. Limit: map-back calls hold only traffic prompts while the main calls are mixed, which may shift descriptions a little.
What each result would mean:
- Traffic-heavy nodes are kinds of prompt (references to missing context, scraped-dataset artefacts, roleplay setups, pressure on rules, chat-log register): an emergent taxonomy reports kind gaps. Kinds that also appear in part 8's list confirm that list; kinds that do not appear there are what a fixed list misses.
- Few traffic-heavy nodes and AMI no higher than the baseline: once the subject is removed, traffic and Dolci differ little in kind at this resolution, and the traffic/Dolci difference is subject or surface text.
- AMI higher than the baseline: the traffic/Dolci difference is carried more by kind of request than by raw text.
- Confirmed topics land in traffic-heavy nodes more often than rejected ones: the loop's confirmation tracks kind gaps (consistent with parts 1-5's template/placeholder result). If not, the loop's gaps are not these kind gaps.
- The median distance of a traffic-heavy node is not above the traffic median: MiniLM distance does not see that kind.
Budget: 200 + 56 (map-back) + 10 (re-description) + 240 (names, both arms) = about 506 Sonnet calls, at most 4 at once (shared subscription); CPU for embeddings and clustering. Checkpoint 1: read the first 40 descriptions before the rest. Checkpoint 2: node table and random members. Checkpoint 3: baseline and map-back. Outputs: `runs/gap_taxonomy/emergent/`, script `scripts/gap_emergent.py`.

## Part 11 (written 2026-10-29 MT before any GPU run): labels from the model, not the text
Callum's first question: a data gap is not the same as a gap in how the model's behaviour is specified; is the model under-specified (high entropy) or untouched by post-training on these prompts? The user's generalization idea asks the same from the other side: did post-training reach this kind of prompt? Question: on the part 8 sample, which prompt kinds (part 8 facets, part 10 nodes) show (a) little change from post-training and (b) high reply entropy, and does that differ between Dolci prompts (trained on) and traffic?
1. Models: Olmo-3-7B-Instruct-DPO (rev b33130b7, the checkpoint used throughout), Olmo-3-7B-Instruct-SFT (rev e1452fc5), Olmo-3-1025-7B (base). bf16, vLLM, one H100. ✓ Same family and tokenizer, so per-token log-probabilities are comparable.
2. Replies: 4 samples per prompt from DPO (temperature 1.0, top_p 1.0, max 256 new tokens, seeds 0-3), prompt in the DPO chat template, prompt text up to 2,000 characters as in part 8. ✓ Temperature 1 samples the model's own distribution, so mean token negative log-probability estimates its entropy rate.
3. Teacher forcing: every sample scored under SFT and base, prompt rendered with the same chat-template string. Per prompt: reach_total = mean over samples and reply tokens of log p_DPO - log p_base (a Monte Carlo estimate of KL(DPO || base) per token); reach_dpo = log p_DPO - log p_SFT; reach_sft = log p_SFT - log p_base; entropy = mean token NLL under DPO. ✓ Output-space diffing needs no activation storage and reads directly as "how far post-training moved the behaviour here".
4. Analysis: per facet and per node, mean of each measure in traffic vs Dolci, and within task type, with reply length as a covariate (KL and NLL scale with length and with refusals). Sanity check first: Dolci prompts (trained on) vs traffic overall.
What each result would mean:
- Reach and entropy do not differ by facet or source beyond task and length: the model-side measure carries no coverage signal here; report and stop.
- Facets rare in Dolci (part 8) show lower reach_total within task: post-training touched those kinds less, the "reverting to the prior" pattern; model-side support that they are real post-training gaps.
- Higher entropy on rare facets within task: behaviour less specified there (Callum's under-specification).
Known confounds: the base model given a chat template is out of its distribution on every prompt (a constant offset, compared across facets only); refusals make reach large; non-English shifts all three. Cost: one H100 for about 40 minutes, about $3 (basis: Runpod H100 about $3/h; 16k samples x 256 tokens plus three teacher-forcing passes). Hard limit 2.5 h; the pod uploads to the private HF dataset detectood/ood and removes itself.

## Part 14b (written 2026-10-29 MT before running): find answer-side gaps over all of Dolci, from words
Question: can a data-only scan of Dolci's training answers find kinds of non-harmful prompt whose answers refuse, at full scale? Pre-set test: it must rediscover NAME_k.
1. All Dolci prompts with their training answer (SFT first assistant turn; DPO chosen), joined as in part 14.
2. Refusal = keyword rule on the answer's first 300 characters (I'm sorry / I can't / I cannot / I won't / As an AI ... with "can't|cannot|unable|not able"), checked against the validated screen on part 14's 1,753 sample answers (agreement and kappa reported before use; used only if kappa >= 0.6).

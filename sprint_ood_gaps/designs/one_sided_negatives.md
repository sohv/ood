# One-sided detectors on ground truth with negatives (2026-10-28, written before any run)

## Question
Stefan (call 2026-10-28): OOD detection may use the training data only. Our gap score and the tf-idf baseline use traffic labels. So the question is: does a detector that sees only Dolci separate prompts of a kind Dolci lacks (positives) from held-out prompts of kinds Dolci has (negatives), and how well? He expects 0.95-0.99 AUROC from good methods on distinct sets. Our earlier distance numbers were 0.64-0.84 (leave-one-source-out, 5 sources).

Earlier negatives evals are not enough for this. The 23-dataset eval scored the gap score, which leaves almost any single new prompt unflagged, positives included (planted legal/medical 0.2% and 2.0% above the line). The negatives there were external datasets, which differ from the positives in writing style (register test). Here negatives and positives come out of the same pool by one rule, so a detector cannot pass by flagging nothing, and style is not confounded with coverage beyond what the source itself carries.

## Step 1 (this design): Dolci-only family hold-out. No traffic.

Chain: question -> choice -> why it measures the question.

1. Ground truth = Dolci's own source labels, grouped into families of the same kind (table below). Removing a family makes it absent by construction; the label does not come from any embedding, so it does not favour a detector built in the same space (planted_word_deleted: deletion by MiniLM neighbours favoured the MiniLM count, deletion by words favoured the rater).
2. Remove a whole family, not one source. Removing Persona MATH alone leaves other math data that refills the neighbour list (shown today for coding, `runs/removal_why/`). That tests partial coverage, which is a second question. Step 1 asks whether the detector sees a kind that is really missing.
3. Exact copies: every reference row whose normalised-text hash equals a removed or held-out row is also dropped. Otherwise a positive or negative can match its own copy and score as covered for a trivial reason. Same rule for both sides.
4. Negatives: 200 random rows per source label with >= 4,000 rows (seed 0), held out of the reference in every run. When family F is removed, negatives from F are not used. They are ID by construction (same sources as the reference, different rows).
5. Positives: 1,000 random rows of family F (seed 0), excluding the held-out negatives.
6. Families split into dev (choose detector settings) and test (report), by a fixed seed before any score. Stefan: which setting wins varies with task; choosing on the same families we report would overstate.
7. Detectors, all fit on the reference only:
   - kNN cosine distance on MiniLM (the existing index, 128-token input): k-th neighbour distance and mean of the top k, k in {1, 5, 20, 50}. k = 1 is our current distance; k-th neighbour distance is the standard kNN OOD score (Sun et al. 2022) and is less sensitive to one stray near-copy.
   - Mahalanobis distance to the reference mean, shared covariance with Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage on a 200,000-row reference sample (384 dimensions; 200k rows gives a stable estimate and keeps the fit fast).
   - Class-conditional Mahalanobis: minimum over Dolci source means with the shared covariance (Lee et al. 2018). Uses training labels only, which is allowed.
   - Length in characters. Stefan's first worry was length; any detector must beat it.
   - Random.
   - Lexical one-sided baseline (tf-idf cosine to the nearest reference prompt) in place of the supervised tf-idf classifier: second pass, if step 1's embedding results leave time.
8. Metric: prompt-level AUROC positives vs negatives per family, with a bootstrap interval; recall at 5% false-positive rate (Stefan prefers precision at the top over averages); AUROC within 4 length quartile bands, so a detector that rides length is visible.
9. Validity per family: median nearest-reference cosine of positives before and after removal. If it barely drops, the family still has look-alikes elsewhere and its result is about partial coverage; report it, do not drop it.

### Size basis
1,000 positives vs about 5,000 negatives per family gives an AUROC standard error of about 0.01 near 0.85. The comparison that matters is family-level (12 families, 6 test): is the mean test AUROC near Stefan's 0.95 or near our 0.64-0.84? A 0.1 difference over 6 families is visible at that n.

### What each result would mean
- Best one-sided detector >= 0.95 on test families: detection is not the bottleneck; move to step 2 with it.
- 0.80-0.90: improving the detector is the priority (Stefan's reading); Sohan's ranking work (activation layers) should plug into this harness.
- Length alone close to the detectors: the families differ mainly in length; report within-band AUROC as primary.

## Step 2 (fixed 2026-10-28 after step 1, before any step-2 score): topic-level split test on traffic

Step 1 result that shapes this: MiniLM one-sided detectors average 0.62-0.71 on whole-family removal; Sohan's source_removal gets 0.88 with Olmo layer-20 activations on a smaller setup. The loop reports topics, and no topic-level ID/OOD ground truth exists yet.

Question: at the unit the loop reports (a topic), does a one-sided detector rank topics that are absent from the reference above topics that are present, and does adding half of a topic to the training side make it stop being flagged while other topics stay where they were?

Stefan's points (28 Oct transcript, Daily Progress doc; 22 Oct notes) -> choice here:
1. "Training data only", no deployment labels -> every detector is fit on the reference alone. The gap score and the supervised tf-idf classifier are not run as methods.
2. "Take every topic, split it, add half, throw away half... can it detect which ones you have thrown away versus added?"; "use all 2,000 topics" -> every pipeline_million topic with enough prompts is split; a seeded half of the topics is ID (their A half goes into the reference), the other half OOD (A half discarded). Both halves' B parts are scored.
3. "Problems about duplications" -> the split is by template group (normalised first 60 characters, the loop's own same-template rule), so one template never sits on both sides; identical texts share a group.
4. "Ignore the train data for starters... designate one as ID and see if your detector distinguishes it" -> V1: reference = ID topics' A halves only (no Dolci). Labels are certain by construction.
5. "Super easy... but definitely should work" -> V1 is a floor. A detector that fails it is broken; passing it is necessary, not sufficient.
6. Realistic version and the iterative point ("does it keep flagging A or switch to B and C") -> V2: reference = all of Dolci, then Dolci + ID topics' A halves. Measure per topic the change in score and how many ID topics leave the natural top 300; OOD topics should not move.
7. "kNN and Mahalanobis at various layers and parameters"; hyperparameters vary by task (22 Oct) -> kNN k in {1, 5, 20} and Mahalanobis on MiniLM here; Olmo activations from Sohan's source_removal pipeline plug into the same query file. The setting is picked on a dev half of topics (seeded) and reported on the test half.
8. Precision over averages ("bring seven real gaps"; precision >= 50%) -> report precision in the top 100 and top 300 topics next to topic-level AUROC.
9. Baselines: bag of words, length, random (22 Oct) -> one-sided tf-idf nearest-reference cosine (replaces the supervised classifier), one-sided length (absolute z-score of log length against the reference), random.
10. Length dominating (his doc) -> length baseline above, and AUROC within topic-length bands.
11. "Don't think about refusals or safety" -> no behaviour endpoint here.

Choices of ours, with reasons:
- Own-space circularity: pipeline_million topics are MiniLM k-means cells, so OOD topics occupy MiniLM regions no ID topic covers, which favours MiniLM detectors (planted_word_deleted showed this for deletion). Second partition: k-means (2,000) on tf-idf -> 256-d SVD vectors. Every detector is reported on both partitions; a detector that is good only on its own partition is reading the partition.
- Class-conditional Mahalanobis with topics as classes is nearest-centroid in the partition's own space; reported but flagged as circular on its own partition.
- Query prompts: up to 25 per topic from the B half (the namer's sample size), excluding traffic prompts that are verbatim in Dolci, because in V2 those are covered by their own copy.
- Topic score = median over its queries (the loop's rule).
- Topics kept: >= 2 template groups and >= 20 non-member prompts in each half after the split; the count dropped is reported.
- Size basis: about 900 ID vs 900 OOD topics gives a topic-level AUROC standard error near 0.01; the comparisons that matter (MiniLM vs activations; own vs other partition) are differences of 0.05 or more.

## Step 4 (written 2026-10-29 before running): unseen same-kind datasets, scored one-sided, with closeness
Stefan (28 Oct call): "negative examples [can be taken] from the datasets that were not even used for training... You got to be careful though about how distinctive they are... sometimes it's very close... slightly different generation... so it's kind of in distribution. Sometimes it's just something completely new." The 23 sets from `designs/negatives_eval.md` were only ever scored with the traffic-labelled gap score, which leaves almost any single new prompt unflagged (planted legal/medical were unflagged too), so that eval could not fail.

Question: under a one-sided detector, how often is each unseen same-kind set flagged at the operating point the traffic ranking uses, compared with held-out Dolci prompts of the same families, and does the flag rate follow how close the set is to Dolci?

Chain:
1. Sets: the 23 `neg_*` sets (1,000 rows each, 100-200 for HarmBench, JBB, knights-and-knaves), exact Dolci members removed by normalised-text hash, as in the original eval. ID floor: the 37 `cov_*` samples of Dolci's own prompts, removed from the reference together with every exact copy (they become held-out Dolci). Reference point (not a ground truth, register-confounded per the register test): planted legal and medical (500 each).
2. Detector: MiniLM kNN distance to Dolci, k = 1 and mean of 5 (the settings used in steps 2-3), embedder and revision as the index (paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2 rev e8f8c211, 128 tokens). Activations are not run here (GPU; the topic split result decides whether they are worth it).
3. Operating point: the 308th-ranked traffic topic's median score under the full Dolci reference (`runs/removal_one_sided/topics.parquet`, full_k1 / full_mean5), the same list length as the removal tests. A prompt is flagged if its score exceeds that value. Reported per set: share flagged, median score, and the same for the held-out Dolci prompts of the set's family.
4. Closeness, measured without MiniLM so it is not the detector reading itself: tf-idf (word 1-2 grams, fit on all Dolci first turns) cosine to the nearest Dolci prompt; per set, the median, and the median against its own Dolci family only. Also the share of rows with a near-copy (tf-idf cosine >= 0.9).
5. Pass rule, carried over from the original eval's bar a (set before that run): a set passes if its flagged share <= max(2 x its Dolci family's held-out flagged share, 2%). Secondary: Spearman between a set's lexical closeness and its flagged share over the 23 sets; a negative correlation is the expected shape (closer sets flagged less), so a set that is far and flagged is "new", not a false positive.
6. Can it fail: yes. A detector that flags everything fails the pass rule; one that flags nothing passes it but then also leaves legal/medical and the removed families (step 1) unflagged, which step 1 already shows it does not. The legal/medical reference point is reported in the same table.

## Step 5 (written 2026-10-29 before computing any candidate score): can a one-sided detector beat one-sided words?

- Candidates, fixed list (no additions after looking); topic score = median over the topic's queries unless stated:

  (b) Mahalanobis on Olmo activations after projecting onto the reference's top 256 principal axes, Ledoit-Wolf covariance in that space (Sohan's `detectors.py` Mahalanobis with pca_dims = 256; the axes are taken from the eigenvectors of the reference covariance instead of an SVD of the full reference matrix, which spans the same subspace and fits in memory), layers 16 / 20 / 24;
  (c) kNN k = 1 on layers 16 + 20 + 24 concatenated, each layer divided by its reference per-dimension std and unit-normalised before concatenation;

- Drop rule: a candidate taking more than 20 minutes on the primary arm is dropped and reported as dropped.

## Step 3b (written 2026-10-28 MT before computing; follows runs/review/step9_design_review_2026-10-28.md)
